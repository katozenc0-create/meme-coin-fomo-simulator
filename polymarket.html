    Metyu knowledge base

    1 · How Polymarket prices work

    Each market has YES and NO shares that settle at $1 or $0 when the question resolves. Because YES + NO ≈ $1, the YES price is the crowd's implied probability. Orders match on a central limit order book; you pay the ask and sell at the bid.

    implied p = YES price · NO ≈ 1 − YES

    2 · Edge is the only reason to trade

    Buying YES at c when the true probability is p earns p − c per share on average. Without a better estimate than the crowd, EV ≈ −spread − fees. Metyu therefore does nothing when it has no information.

    EV = p − c (per $1 share)

    3 · Fair value = market + evidence

    The market price and private research are two noisy estimates of the same truth. Metyu combines them by precision (inverse variance) in log-odds space and tracks changing evidence with a Kalman filter. Information arrives faster as resolution nears, so the filter trusts old evidence less over time — otherwise it bets against news the crowd already priced.

    x̂ = (x_m/σ²_m + x_r/σ²_r) / (1/σ²_m + 1/σ²_r)

    4 · Size with fractional Kelly

    Kelly maximises long-run growth when p is known exactly. Estimates are never exact, so Metyu uses ¼ Kelly and caps each position. Full Kelly roughly halves your bankroll on a routine bad streak.

    f* = (p − c) / (1 − c)

    5 · Demand edge above uncertainty

    A 4-point edge with ±6 points of uncertainty is noise. Metyu only trades when edge exceeds max(min edge, k·σ). Sitting out is a position.

    trade if edge > max(minEdge, k·σ_p)

    6 · Leverage on binaries

    A binary can gap to $0 on news. With leverage L, a move of about 1/L against you erases the margin — at 10x, a 5-cent gap on a 50¢ share is a liquidation. Leverage multiplies a real edge and the drawdown; without edge it only speeds up ruin. Metyu scales leverage with edge/volatility, never above your cap, and never holds leverage into resolution. (Run the backtest at low research accuracy to see it.)

    liq price = borrowed / (shares · (1 − maintenance))

    7 · Exits that respect EV

    On an unlevered binary, a price stop sells a still-positive-EV share at a loss. Metyu exits when the edge is gone, when leverage approaches liquidation, or when the thesis breaks — and a drawdown kill-switch halts trading entirely.

    exit if fair − mark ≤ 0

    8 · Costs and liquidity

    The spread, taker fees and thin books are certain costs; edge is uncertain. Large orders walk the book. Low-liquidity markets can show a price you cannot actually trade at size.

    net edge = edge − spread/2 − fee

    9 · Resolution and calibration

    Markets resolve through an oracle process (Polymarket uses UMA's optimistic oracle) that can be disputed; read each market's rules. Judge forecasts with the Brier score: lower is better, 0.25 is a coin flip.

    Brier = mean((forecast − outcome)²)

    No agent can guarantee profit. In real markets edge is rare, usually small, and decays as others find it. Rules that survive: no edge → no trade; size small; leverage rarely and briefly; plan the exit before entry.
